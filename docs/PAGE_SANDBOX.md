# The webpage is the arena

Demo Day's primary play path starts on the actual webpage. `Wreck this page` offers Survivor 616 or Kinetic Souls. The standalone full games and the smaller media editor are secondary entry points.

## Layer contract

1. The live website stays underneath. Games may read layout rectangles and computed styles to locate targets, but must not remove, rewrite, or submit page content.
2. A viewport-sized full-resolution layer paints reversible damage over page elements. A separate low-resolution canvas is scaled with nearest-neighbor rendering for actors and pixel effects. This is the layered rendering technique described by [Sprite Fusion](https://www.spritefusion.com/blog/pixel-art-rendering-trick-for-a-90s-look).
3. A temporary input layer prevents gameplay clicks from activating links or buttons. Restore removes all game layers and listeners and returns the original scroll position.
4. Each game's page adapter belongs in its game repository. Survivor's adapter uses its world engine, character roster, waves, and DOM scanner. Kinetic's adapter selects Classic or KS2 fighter, weapon, AI, rendering, modifier, collision, and page-target behavior. Demo Day pins reviewed builds of both.

## Expansion points

- A shared cross-game target contract should expose bounding boxes, material/type, hit points, damage state, and rewards. Kinetic's page adapter now uses one target collection for DOM targets, starter props, and private media; projecting these tools into Survivor without changing its core loop remains future work.
- The page selector can place a private image or MP4/WebM/Ogg video as a live DOM target. Kinetic's in-match Creative Sandbox adds its own physics-driven props and media to that same target collection. Files use session-only object URLs unless saved to the existing local IndexedDB scene store.
- Kinetic Classic and KS2 use the same page adapter, with their original character, weapon, physics-damage, and arena-background renderers selected by version. The default page arena uses the original Synthwave background and bouncy movement. When fighters or shots hit a page target, cracks open to that animated arena, impact flashes and recoil show where force landed, and a break throws color-matched fragments that spin, fall, and bounce. These effects stay on the reversible overlay; the site's DOM remains intact.
- The existing left and right team spawn buttons and keyboard A/L launch once on tap, then repeat every 150 ms while held. Pointer release, keyup, cancellation, or page restoration stops the repeats.
- The in-page Kinetic modifier catalog uses the source games' actual visible theme, modifier, environment, and weapon settings plus source Bestiary data. Gravity Well, Time Distortion, and Chaos Vortex are labeled page-mode additions. KS2 also adapts Solar Flares, Magnetic Fields, and Void Storm to the page world and labels them as such. KOs have native burst feedback, while the page UI follows Kinetic's monospace HUD, team colors, orange/purple Classic accents, and violet/cyan KS2 accents.
- Kinetic Classic and KS2 page matches now run for two minutes. Player 1 spawns on the left and faces the right-side NPC; right-side input hands that team to Player 2 for 12 seconds. The NPC resumes after the timeout only when enabled in Settings. Match completion reports the selected version, duration, team KO scores, result, and run ID; no currency is granted.
- Game-choice cards and their preview canvases carry `data-lok-nondestructible`. Kinetic and Survivor scanners also reject `.lok-choice` descendants, so selectors never become page obstacles or collision targets.
- The in-match Creative Sandbox adds starter props and private image/video files to Kinetic's same target collection as the webpage. Props can be moved, collide with fighters, take combat damage, and reveal the selected arena when destroyed. The panel can be minimized without pausing play. It supports rebuild, local IndexedDB save/open, video scrubbing, paused fighter-position replay scrubbing, arena still export, and an eight-second WebM capture when the browser supports `MediaRecorder`. Removing page mode deletes only props created by the session and revokes their object URLs.
- Kinetic page targets now progress through cracked, breaking, and unstable damage stages. The largest eligible piece becomes a one-run Rift Warden with triple target health, a visible health readout, and a nine-second pulse that damages nearby page pieces. Breaking a piece triggers a bounded chain blast against up to four nearby targets; defeating the Warden expands that blast to eight targets and a wider radius. Chain blasts do not recursively trigger more blasts, keeping frame cost and cascade size bounded. No page text or controls are changed.
- Kinetic destruction now adds impact shock rings, bigger break and boss rings, and a timed ×2-to-×8 break combo. Combos widen bounded chain blasts and throw larger, more energetic color-matched shards, while floating labels call out the streak. Shock rings respect reduced-motion preferences; the original webpage remains reversible.
- Survivor 616 already routes its native world impacts through the page adapter: breaking a block flashes and dissolves it, throws pixel debris, can open a crater into its map scenery, and applies the game's hit-stop and combo feedback. It keeps its own fixed-step world physics.
- The separate Studio remains available for webpage fields, YouTube's official player, and its broader authoring workflow. The in-match Kinetic panel applies its scene and media actions to the live arena rather than replacing the live page with a small studio canvas.
- Streams, sponsor objects, and approved rewarded placements can add bounded targets through the same contract. They must not make ordinary display ads clickable combat targets.
- Completion events carry versioned game ID, run ID, score, and cosmetic XP to the account bridge. Competitive or spendable rewards need a trusted receipt.

The original [`Destroy Any Website`](https://destroy.spritefusion.com/) inspired the website-sized play field. Demo Day's live-page overlay is its own implementation and uses no Sprite Fusion code or art.

Kinetic matches support 1, 2, 3, or 5 minutes, defaulting to two minutes. A changed length applies on rematch, Shrinking Arena follows the active timer, and validated run details include elapsed and configured duration. The next Kinetic page-mode work is tracked in [Kinetic Souls page-mode parity](KINETIC_PAGE_PARITY.md), including the source-of-truth rule, version-specific settings, and release gates.
